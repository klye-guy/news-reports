## ITEM 1: Aviation — Duffy: L3Harris Copper-to-Fiber Router "Flooded" ATC Network; Root Cause Not Yet Established; More Than 2,500 Saturday Cancellations; Miami Taxiway Collision

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: At a press conference Saturday, 14 NOV, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said that "in at least one of our remote FAA locations, a contractor was doing routine maintenance to switch telecommunication lines from old copper wire" to fiber, and that the contractor is L3Harris, which "actually owns that network." He said the router being installed, "either through contractor error or a faulty piece of equipment," began "flooding other routers in our air traffic control system," with "over 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds," disrupting communications with aircraft and some radar feeds (DOT transcript). "What we're working to determine was whether we had incompetence, meaning was it user error or install error from L3Harris or did they install a faulty router? We haven't determined what the fault was." On a cyberattack: "We don't believe so. Once we were able to isolate the work that was being done at this remote location, all of our systems came back online" (DOT). FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said there was "zero evidence" of anything "nefarious" or AI-related (CNBC), but CNBC reported the root cause "remains unclear"; Bedford said, "Because of our lack of identifying the root cause, we were unwilling to take any risk," and "We couldn't explain the instability." Officials cited products and services from Cisco and L3Harris (CNBC). Duffy: "Until I can build [the new architecture], I'm stuck with L3Harris, who owns this network." Acting FAA CTO Rebecca Guy said, "at this time, we do believe what occurred with that router has been worked through" (NPR). Officials told Reuters there may have been problems with routers in both Arkansas and Texas (via USA Today). Fox News reported that Duffy said the FBI and intelligence officials, including FBI Director Kash Patel on an emergency call, examined whether a foreign actor was involved. L3Harris said it is "supporting the FAA in its investigation" and that it and partners have completed more than 70% of the copper-to-fiber conversion; DOT put replaced copper at 69%. DOT said controllers grounded more than 3,500 airborne flights and more than 8,000 operations resumed Friday night. More than 2,500 US flights were canceled Saturday (Star Tribune, with AP). USA Today described the stop as about five hours, with nearly 10,000 cancellations and more than 7,000 delays. Separately, at about 9:15 p.m. Saturday (ET as reported; about 2015 CST), a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 taxiing to its gate and a UPS Boeing 767 taxiing for departure collided at a Miami International Airport taxiway intersection. Copa's left wingtip struck the UPS nose; two passengers had minor injuries, and the FAA is investigating (ABC; CBS Miami, which reported 172 people aboard the Copa jet; Simple Flying reported UPS had been told to give way). This is a ground-handling incident with no reported link to the ATC failure.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: The government's working explanation places blame on a single contractor network at a remote site, which raises questions about why one router could destabilize the national airspace and about the FAA's dependence on L3Harris. Expect lame-duck and incoming-Congress oversight of the contract and the modernization money Duffy requested ($50 billion, carried). Weekend recovery is under way, but Thanksgiving travel starts in about 10 days.
