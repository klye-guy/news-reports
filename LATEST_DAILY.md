## ITEM 1: Hormuz — UKMTO Reports Vessel Afire North of Khasab; IRGC Says Three Tankers Hit Mines on the "Unauthorized" Southern Route; IRGC Claims Capture of US MQM-170 Drone

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: UKMTO said the master of a vessel reported seeing another vessel on fire about 9 NM north of Khasab, Oman, in the Strait of Hormuz at about 0300 GMT on 29 OCT; crew status was unknown, there was no damage or environmental assessment, authorities were investigating, and ships were told to transit with caution (CBS/AFP; Xinhua; Anadolu; Arab News). The UKMTO warning number was not found. On 29 OCT the IRGC Navy said three oil tankers "exploded and caught fire" after striking naval mines over the past 24 hours while trying to exit through "the unauthorized southern route," that they had switched off radar and navigation systems, ignored IRGC warnings, and were stranded along the southern shore; it held owners and crews responsible (CBS; AP headline; Al Jazeera). The US has urged shippers to use the southern lane, which Trump declared mine-free at the end of August (CBS). The tankers were not named, and only one burning vessel has been reported by UKMTO. Separately, the IRGC Navy said its electronic warfare unit took control of and "safely landed" a US MQM-170 reconnaissance drone west of the strait, claiming it had launched from a southern Gulf state (CBS). No US response to either claim was found. This follows Iran's threat of "legal action" against Sinokor vessels starting 01 NOV.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: If confirmed, mine strikes on the US-endorsed southern lane would undercut the CENTCOM mine-clearance claim and raise insurance and freight costs on the crude still moving through Hormuz. It also hands Washington a possible trigger for action days before the post-midterm window. Watch for a US Navy statement, vessel identities, and new UKMTO or JMIC advisories.
