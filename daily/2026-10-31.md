## ITEM 3: Hormuz — UKMTO 187-26 Is the Louise 1 Limpet Attack, Separate From 186-26; Burning "Vessel" Was a Dhow; Iran Says US Drone Hit Fishing Boats Off Qeshm

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: UKMTO Warning 187-26 Update 001 covers the attack about 25 NM northeast of Khasab at about 2212 UTC on 29 OCT (1712 CDT), in which a small boat approached on the port side and attached two explosive devices above the waterline, causing an engine-room fire; the fire is out, the crew safe, and the vessel continuing on (Public TV citing UKMTO). Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech identified the tanker as the Panama-flagged Louise 1, a 159,092 dwt tanker owned by Panama-based Aquarius Maritime Ventures (Seatrade Maritime). Warning 186-26, a projectile strike at 1600 UTC on 29 OCT on the southern corridor near Oman, is a separate incident, so two ships were hit within about six hours. UKMTO also said the vessel reported burning north of Khasab early on 29 OCT was a regional trading dhow that sank after a fire, with its crew evacuated. Seatrade cites IMO figures of 34 confirmed attacks on vessels between 28 SEP and 27 OCT. UKMTO counted 155 inbound and 122 outbound Hormuz transits in the week ending 28 OCT, about 70% below pre-war levels (Al Jazeera). Iranian state TV claimed late on 30 OCT that at least six tankers, including a VLCC carrying two million barrels, were damaged by mines on "unauthorised" routes, and aired small-boat footage of a burning ship (Al Jazeera). On 31 OCT Iranian state media said a US drone attacked two fishing boats off Qeshm Island, wounding two fishermen; two missing fishermen were later found. AFP asked CENTCOM for comment; no US response was found (Dawn/AFP; Xinhua; Business Recorder). In a video on 30 OCT, Adm. Cooper said he flew over the strait and "didn't see the IRGC out there at all," adding that Iran's navy "is all at the bottom of the sea" (Al Jazeera).

A US strike on Iranian fishing boats, if confirmed, would give Tehran a grievance just as talks move to Doha.

## ITEM 6: Elections and Justice — Ninth Circuit Rejects DOJ California Ballot Bid 2–1; Judge Bars DOJ and DHS From Intimidating Election Officials Through 16 NOV; DOJ Amends Alaska Suit

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: On 30 OCT a Ninth Circuit panel denied 2–1 DOJ's emergency request to make California segregate mail ballots from more than 20,000 voters DOJ says may be noncitizens. Judges Mark Bennett (Trump appointee) and Gabriel Sanchez (Biden appointee) wrote that "there is a material risk that granting the United States its requested relief would result in disenfranchisement" and found "no feasible way to identify and segregate the ballots of specific individuals without potentially catastrophic effects." Judge Patrick Bumatay dissented: "We should have let them try" (NBC; Reuters; Sacramento Bee). Reuters says this could set up a Supreme Court showdown; no filing was found. The underlying suit continues before Judge Carter, who appointed a special master. Hours later, US District Judge Trina Thompson in California issued a temporary restraining order through 16 NOV barring DOJ and DHS from actions that "threaten, intimidate or coerce" election officials to halt processing ballots solely because the voter is alleged to be ineligible, writing that "high-ranking DOJ and DHS officials have engaged in a campaign to intimidate" officials (NBC; CNN). NBC reports DOJ sent letters to election officials in 57 of 58 California counties last week; First Assistant US Attorney Bilal Essayli's San Bernardino letter listed "1133 likely aliens," asked for a 48-hour commitment, and warned that "the legal consequences for either facilitating or ignoring federal crimes are significant." A similar Washington State case is pending. In Alaska, DOJ amended its civil suit seeking to block certification if Sen. Sullivan loses, calling the alleged same-name recruitment a "conspiracy," and a DOJ official cited 18 U.S.C. 241 and 242 as possible criminal statutes while acknowledging no known case of a voter mistakenly choosing the wrong Dan Sullivan (CBS). Peltola's campaign and the DSCC deny involvement.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: With three court losses this week and a TRO in place, California will count the disputed ballots unless the Supreme Court intervenes before 03 NOV. The Alaska suit and criminal probe set up a certification fight if Peltola wins.
