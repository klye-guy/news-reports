## ITEM 1: Hormuz and Gulf Shipping — Record Attack Tempo, RPG Strike off Al Jubayl, IRGC Mine Claim

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: BBC Verify (16 OCT) analysis of UKMTO data: 12 vessels attacked 5–11 OCT, the largest weekly total since the US–Israeli war with Iran began (prior week 10); Kpler data put Hormuz flows at about 12.6 million barrels per day on 13 OCT, down from ~15 million at end-September and vs a pre-war baseline of ~17 million; Orbital Risk Intelligence CEO Martin Kelly said Iran is carrying out "near daily or multiple attacks every single day" and widening targeting toward Gulf Arab coasts and the Gulf of Oman. CBS live coverage (updated 17 OCT 0637 ET) cited UKMTO for at least 10 attacks since last Sunday and 23 since the start of October (almost double the average), making October the busiest month since March; UKMTO said it is "struggling to accurately report" the true number because most ships now refuse to report; most attacks since early July have occurred off Oman, where the US military guides at-risk vessels. New incidents: UKMTO Warning 175-26 (primary PDF, issued 17 OCT): 16 OCT 1922 UTC, 78 NM northeast of Al Jubayl, Saudi Arabia, a tanker master reported an RPG attack from a small boat damaging the aft port quarter; crew accounted for and safe; damage and environmental impact unknown. CBS also reported an earlier 16 OCT UKMTO report of a tanker struck by an "unknown projectile" off Saudi Arabia, crew evacuated, no environmental impact reported (warning number not confirmed this cycle). Iran's navy (state media via Reuters/Al Arabiya, 16 OCT) said it "detected" a "violating" tanker on fire in a prohibited area east of Hormuz in the Gulf of Oman after it switched off tracking, without saying what caused the fire. Early 17 OCT the IRGC claimed a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels, "attempting to pass illegally... with U.S. support," struck a mine and caught fire; no location given (CBS/AFP) — see quarantine. Iranian rhetoric: army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia (X, 16 OCT): "If the United States leaves the region, the Strait of Hormuz will reopen... If it does not leave, we will expel it"; Rear Adm. Habibollah Sayyari and Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami (Tehran Times, 16 OCT) said all passage must be coordinated with Iran and the strait stays closed "until Iran's rights" are respected. Separately, Iraq's foreign minister said the war has cost Iraq about $60 billion in oil export revenue (AFP via CBS, 15–16 OCT).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: Attacks deep inside the Gulf (Al Jubayl is far from the strait) and a likely undercount by UKMTO mean war-risk premiums and freight costs stay high even where US escorts operate; this is a direct input to WTI's move through $100 (Item 4) and to record diesel at the pump. The gap between the administration's "strait is open" line and Iranian enforcement claims is now the central election-season energy question.
