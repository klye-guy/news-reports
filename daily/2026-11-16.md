## ITEM 4: Aviation — FAA Names Two Sites (El Dorado, Ark., and Amarillo, Texas); Broadcasting Function Disabled on New Routers; ~12,462 Friday Cancellations; 2,070+ More Sunday; Duffy: Normal by Monday

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: The FAA's Sunday 15 NOV Q&A says the legacy equipment "became unstable when data began flooding the FAA FTI network, at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds." The initial investigation points to "two potential locations": "a radar site in Amarillo, Texas and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas," where L3Harris, "the FAA's current network service provider," had been deploying a new router. L3Harris blocked both locations and the network stabilized, and "the broadcasting function was also disabled on all the new routers as a precaution." Instability hit 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers and spread into several TRACONs. The FAA says "we have no indication that this was a cyberattack," that root-cause investigation continues with L3Harris, and that operators canceled "approximately 12,462 flights" Friday, "including 9,082 cancellations by major airlines," with "not all cancellations... directly caused by the nationwide ground stop" (FAA, read directly). On NBC's Meet the Press, Duffy said the El Dorado remote radio facility's line was being decommissioned by the telecom provider, and "when we turned this facility on... the router we used was flooding the rest of our network." He said the routers being flooded "were purchased in 2001. They were obsolete in 2006." He still does not know whether it was "a faulty installation by L3Harris" or "a fault of the router itself," said he "can't" cut ties because L3Harris "own[s] the network," and expects air travel "back to normal tomorrow." On cyber, he said no agency "has seen... indications that this was a foreign actor." On CNN, he said the network architecture "is from 1980" (via syndicated wire). DHS Secretary Mullin said the FAA failure and the Microsoft outage "are not connected whatsoever" (Fox). ## ITEM 5: Technology — Microsoft Preliminary Post-Incident Report: Latent July Network-Gateway Defect; 80%+ of Gateway Servers Unresponsive at Worst; Final Report Due 20 NOV

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: The Register reports that Microsoft's preliminary post-incident report, published 15 NOV, traces the 12–14 NOV Exchange Online and Microsoft 365 outage to a defect in a July 2026 update for Microsoft 365 network gateway servers. Microsoft wrote: "Our load testing tended to focus on weaker hardware with fewer cores." The defect caused "lock contention inside an internal Windows process" that exhausted CPU and memory. It was triggered when a Teams failover at 0107 UTC 12 NOV added load while "almost 14 percent" of gateway capacity was out of service. The failure cascaded until "more than 80 percent of servers were unresponsive." Rollback was not possible because the defect was months old, so engineers applied "an update that returned the code path to the previously known good state." Exchange Online could not fail out of the gateway "for scale reasons." The mitigation was applied at 0720 UTC 13 NOV, and health was confirmed at 1610 UTC 13 NOV. A final report is due 20 NOV (The Register).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: This adds detail to Microsoft's 13 NOV "network capacity upgrade" statement (carried) and still rules out an attack. For US enterprises, the lesson is that hyperscaler change-management risk can stay latent for months. Watch for service-credit decisions and the 20 NOV final report.
