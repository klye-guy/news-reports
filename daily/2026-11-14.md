## ITEM 1: Aviation — FAA Nationwide Ground Stop of About Four Hours After ATC Communications Failure; About 10,000 Cancellations; No Evidence of Cyberattack So Far

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: On Friday, 13 NOV, the FAA "issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers" (FAA statement). Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the trouble began at 3:36 p.m. (about 1436 CST, assuming ET as reported), when the centers saw instability in radar and communications systems: "Our switches, our routers actually got overloaded with data and they started to give us inconsistencies in the data from those routers and because of that we shut the system down." He said radar and communications were never completely lost and planes landed safely. Airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. ET (1915 CST), with departures resuming gradually: "It's going to be slow" (Reuters). Reuters called it the longest shutdown since the 11 SEP 2001 attacks; the only other nationwide ground stop since then was about 90 minutes in January 2023. By 11:30 p.m. nearly 10,000 flights had been canceled, including more than 2,200 each at Delta and American and 1,800 at United (Flightradar24 via Reuters). Fox News reported more than 7,000 flights delayed or canceled while the stop was still in effect, and TimcastNews reported that Eurocontrol suspended Europe-to-US departures at the FAA's request (single source; see quarantine). The Star Tribune said the problem affected communications between controllers and about 3,500 aircraft in the air across all 22 regional centers. Duffy and Mullin said the problems surfaced when new technology being installed was integrated with the old system (Star Tribune), and two airline officials told Reuters the affected network, which carries controller-pilot radio frequencies, is overseen by L3Harris (L3Harris did not comment). Mullin said there is "zero evidence" so far of a cyberattack or a rogue AI system but neither is ruled out, that "It appears this was mechanically driven," and that NSA is working with DHS. On a possible link to the Microsoft outage, Mullin said, "I don't see any reason to connect those two at all" (Fox News). Duffy called for $50 billion to rebuild the system; earlier Friday the White House and DOT had asked Congress for $30.1 billion for airports and ATC (Reuters). Airlines are not required to compensate stranded passengers because the FAA caused the failure. AAL, UAL and DAL fell 1% to 2% after hours, and JetBlue about 3% (Reuters). At MSP, 126 of 428 Friday departures were canceled (Cirium via Star Tribune), the Gophers football team could not leave for Saturday's game at Penn State, and the airport said it would take a day or more to clear the backlog. A search summary of the MSP flights page Saturday morning showed 43 departures and 80 arrivals canceled (not opened directly).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: A single communications failure halted the national airspace system for four hours, with recovery stretching into the weekend and the Thanksgiving travel run-up. Expect a push in the lame-duck Congress for ATC modernization money and questions about the L3Harris network and the integration of new equipment.
- SOURCE SYNOPSIS: FAA statement (13 NOV, read directly); Reuters (13 NOV, read directly); Aviation Week (13 NOV, partial read); Star Tribune (13 NOV, read directly); AP (headlines/search excerpts; pages timed out); Fox News and TheBlaze X posts quoting Duffy and Mullin (13–14 NOV).
