## ITEM 1: Midterms — Democrats Win Control of the House and Senate; Seven Senate Flips; Major Governor Gains

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: In the 03 NOV midterm elections, Democrats won majorities in both chambers of Congress, NBC News projected (live blog updated 0647 CST 04 NOV). NBC said Democrats flipped seven Senate seats (Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Texas) and will win at least 258 House seats; BBC reported a projection of 245 Democratic and 166 Republican House seats; Ballotpedia, as of 0030 CST, put the floor at 231–141 in the House and 54–44 in the Senate with counting ongoing. Ballotpedia listed the Senate winners: Josh Turek (Iowa, over Ashley Hinson), Adam Hamilton (Kansas, over incumbent Roger Marshall), Troy Jackson (Maine, over incumbent Susan Collins, who conceded), Roy Cooper (North Carolina, over Michael Whatley), Sherrod Brown (Ohio special, over incumbent Jon Husted), Annie Andrews (South Carolina, over incumbent Darline Graham), and James Talarico (Texas, over Ken Paxton). Democrat Abdul El-Sayed beat Republican Mike Rogers in Michigan's Senate race and is described by Fox News as the first Muslim elected to the Senate. Ballotpedia said 14 Republican House incumbents and no Democrats lost re-election. NBC said Democrats flipped governorships in Texas, Georgia, Nevada, Ohio, and Iowa; Texas Gov. Greg Abbott congratulated Democrat Gina Hinojosa (Fox News). Fox News projected Sen. Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) won a full term. AP via Fox News reported West Virginia approved a US-citizenship-to-vote amendment and Ohio a constitutional voter ID requirement. NBC's exit poll cited economic frustration, shifts among Latino voters and Black men, and rejection of Trump; Fox News's voter poll said young, independent, rural, and Hispanic voters moved away from the GOP.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: Divided government begins in January. Democrats will control committee subpoenas, appropriations, and Senate confirmations; the Washington Post expects a wave of investigations of the administration. War-powers votes on Iran, tariffs, immigration enforcement, and AI regulation are the likeliest early clashes, with Trump's veto as the backstop. Seat totals are still moving and should be treated as projections.

## ITEM 3: Iran Diplomacy — Rezaei Calls US Reply "Not Convincing"; Qatar Facilitating "Real-Time Exchange of Proposals"; No Deadline, Says Iranian Official

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: On Tuesday, 03 NOV, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, wrote on X: "America's response to Iran's rights and demands was not convincing, and they must fully and explicitly address Iran's seven conditions and act upon them. Only in this case will the Strait of Hormuz be reopened" (Al Jazeera; Reuters via Korea Herald/CNA). Qatar's foreign ministry spokesman Majed al-Ansari said both sides "remain committed to the negotiation process," that Qatar is facilitating the "real-time exchange of proposals," and "If there were no glimmer of hope, there would be no negotiations" (Al Jazeera; Reuters). An official briefed on the talks said feedback was expected within 36 hours; a senior Iranian official later told Reuters there is no deadline for exchanging messages, that Tehran will not accept Washington's "excessive demands," and that nuclear talks can begin only after Iran's conditions are met. Iran's seven-step plan, as described by spokesman Baghaei, has the US end its port blockade, Iran reopen Hormuz, a US waiver on Iranian oil sales, cancellation of new sanctions, and then nuclear talks (Sudan Independent, citing Iranian statements). Trump told the Financial Times "We seem to be getting everything we want" (Al Jazeera).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: Rezaei's rejection pushes back on reports of progress, but neither side has walked away. The 36-hour feedback window from Monday night runs into Wednesday, the same day US media say strike plans could begin (Item 4). A deal would lower oil and gasoline prices; a collapse raises strike risk.

## ITEM 10: Minnesota — Klobuchar Wins Governor, Flanagan Wins Senate, DFL Wins Trifecta; MN-1 Headed for Recount; Omar Re-Elected
