## ITEM 1: Iran Strike Track — CNN: US Ready for Major Strikes "Within 96 Hours" of an Order; Trump Names Pickaxe Mountain; Israel Told to Wait for Joint Operations

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: CNN (26 OCT) reports, citing two US officials, that US forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch new, potentially major operations against Iran "within 96 hours of receiving" an order from Trump, and that sufficient forces are now in the region. The scope would likely go beyond strikes on radars and launchers; CENTCOM and the Pentagon have built a target list including Iranian energy infrastructure, military sites, and nuclear facilities. One official said, "It will be a big effort." Officials say Trump has not decided but "appears to be leaning toward ordering major action," most likely after the midterms and possibly any time before year's end. A senior Pentagon official said, "The Department is a planning organization." CNN also reports nonessential US troops due in the next rotation will no longer be sent, and that other US embassies in the region are expected to get departure orders or personnel caps. On Israel, CNN cites two US officials and an Israeli security source that US officials asked Netanyahu to hold off on new strikes until joint operations are ready; Netanyahu's office called the pre-election joint-attack account "fake news." Israel's Channel 12 reported a confrontation between IDF chief Zamir and Netanyahu over an Iran-related directive; Defense Minister Katz called it "completely baseless and false" (CNN). In his Fox News interview, Trump said, "It's getting to be decision time," and named Pickaxe Mountain, a deeply buried nuclear-linked site: "We know exactly what's going on at Pickaxe Mountain" (CNBC). CNBC notes the last confirmed US strikes on Iran were on 01 SEP. A White House official told Fox News Digital, "We do not comment on sensitive military plans." NPR reports Iran says it is prepared to go back to war.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: The reporting points to a short-notice, large-scale option ready for the post-election window, and the troop-rotation pause and expected embassy cuts are practical signs of preparation. CNN's intelligence sources expect Iran and the Houthis to retaliate immediately against US personnel and facilities if struck, so the risk to Americans in the region rises sharply the moment an order is given.

## ITEM 2: Iran Diplomacy — First Iran–Saudi–Pakistan–Turkey Meeting Ends With Agreement to Keep Talking; Munir Proposal; Qatar's Five-Day Hormuz-for-Blockade Plan; Bessent Tells Iranians to Withhold Taxes

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: On 26 OCT in Tehran, SNSC secretary Mohsen Rezaei hosted Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir, and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin; President Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Araghchi also attended. A joint statement said the four "agreed to continue the dialogue to strengthen cooperation amongst the four regional countries, including for peace and security in the region" and set no date for a next meeting (Press TV via Al Jazeera; Arab News Pakistan). Rezaei called the talks "very good" and said regional security "should be achieved by the countries themselves" without "foreign intervention" (Straits Times; Daily Sabah). A senior Pakistani official told Reuters the agenda covered a regional nonaggression network, US–Iran negotiations, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, Hormuz, and the US blockade of Iranian ports; another said Munir, "with US President Donald Trump's blessing to pursue diplomacy," presented a proposal offering something to both sides, which the Mecca pact states and Qatar could guarantee (Reuters via Al Jazeera). The NYT reports Qatar is working on a five-day reciprocal deal in which Iran reopens Hormuz to commercial shipping and the US lifts its port blockade, with sequencing the main dispute; Iranian state-linked reporting describes a seven-day phased plan tied to access to $24 billion in reserves (NYT via Iran International). An X summary says Iran will not discuss nuclear issues until sanctions, the blockade, and asset freezes are addressed. In an Iran International interview on 26 OCT, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent advised Iranians not to pay taxes and to stock up on heating fuel and gasoline ahead of expected winter shortages, and predicted Iran's economy will contract more than 25% between March 2026 and March 2027 (Iran International).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: The meeting produced no deal but kept a channel open that Washington appears to endorse through Munir. A Hormuz-for-blockade swap is the most concrete off-ramp in view and would ease oil prices quickly if agreed; Iran's refusal to put nuclear issues first is the main obstacle. Bessent's remarks show the economic pressure campaign running in parallel with strike planning.
- SOURCE SYNOPSIS: Al Jazeera (26 and 27 OCT, search summaries citing Press TV and Reuters); Arab News Pakistan (26 OCT); Straits Times (AFP); Daily Sabah; Iran International (NYT five-day plan; Bessent interview); X News summary (nuclear precondition).
