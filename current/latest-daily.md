## ITEM 1: Hormuz — GFS Pride Sinks, Iran Tells Reuters It Changed Tactics to Sway US Vote; Attack Tempo at War High

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: Singapore MPA (primary release, 14 OCT): Singapore-registered container ships GFS PRIDE and GFS GISELLE were “reportedly struck by unknown projectiles while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on 13 and 14 October 2026 (Singapore Time)”; GFS PRIDE “sustained damage and a fire on board, and has since sunk. All 21 crew members were safely evacuated”; GFS GISELLE sustained hull damage and reached a safe anchorage under its own propulsion with all 22 crew safe. Both are operated by Dubai-based GFS Ship Management (Straits Times). BBC: the Hydrographer of Pakistan placed the wreck 4 nm north-west of Ras al-Sheikh Masud, Musandam (Oman). The IRGC Navy claimed a container ship carrying 2,500+ containers sank after hitting a naval mine on an “unauthorized and hazardous route”; a US official told ABC Iran lied and attacked the ship, likely with a missile. UKMTO Warning 173-26 (primary PDF): 13 OCT 1700 UTC, a 19,000-tonne container ship struck by an unknown projectile on an outbound transit, minor damage, no casualties, continued passage. Vanguard (via CBS/AFP) named Tuesday's four struck vessels as GFS Pride, GFS Giselle, the Barbados-flagged tanker Alfonso (fate unknown) and the Marshall Islands-flagged Al Saadiyat; it is not established which UKMTO numbers map to which ship. Separately, the Antigua and Barbuda-flagged bulk carrier Cape Dao sank near the strait weeks after a 23 SEP strike that killed one crew member (IMO data via Reuters/gCaptain). Reuters (Hafezi/Saul/Stewart, 14 OCT): two senior Iranian officials said Tehran began “different offensive tactics” about 10 days ago, decided “at the highest levels,” partly to influence the US vote; “Just when they think they are getting close to the point where prices will fall, we will push them away from it”; operations will widen to the Gulf of Oman and beyond; “It will be complete closure soon.” A US military official confirmed the IRGC now uses unarmed spotters in small boats after US strikes destroyed much of Iran's radar, and that US forces made a dhow's occupants abandon ship and destroyed it last week with no casualties; CENTCOM announced the dhow strike Wednesday (Gulf of Oman; crew “safely removed”; custody status unclear per CBS). Counts: Vanguard/IMO/UKMTO data show at least 10 vessels attacked since Sunday 11 OCT and 23 attacks since 01 OCT, almost double the war's monthly average; Vanguard says Hormuz traffic had recovered to about 80% of pre-war levels (AFP via CBS). Greek monitor Marisks (15 OCT): three ships struck Tuesday were running with AIS off, and three ships struck this week share one operator, raising “possible operator-linked targeting.” Iranian Army Rear Adm. Habibollah Sayyari (IRNA, 15 OCT): “The Strait of Hormuz is closed; if it were not closed... gas prices in the United States would not have increased.” JMIC (Sun 11 OCT) kept the threat at “severe,” second-highest.
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: Iran is openly stating that the attacks are aimed at US pump prices before 03 NOV, so expect the tempo to hold or rise through the election regardless of the no-strike pledge. A sinking raises war-risk insurance and could thin the US-assisted Oman-coast traffic that CENTCOM credits for the 19M-barrel day reported 12 OCT. Spotter-dhow engagements show US forces are already using force against IRGC support craft below the threshold of resumed strikes.
