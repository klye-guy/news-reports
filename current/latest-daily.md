- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: Following the WSJ's 23 NOV report (prior daily, Item 2), a Coast Guard spokesperson told Fox News Digital on 23 NOV that Coast Guard cyber teams with FBI Cyber Action Team operators boarded two inbound foreign-flagged ships in the Gulf of America on 21 and 24 AUG after "indications that the networks of both vessels were compromised by foreign cyber actors." The threats were mitigated before the ships reached port. The FBI and Coast Guard told NPR there are no current "reports of operational disruptions, vessel instability, physical danger to crews, or environmental impacts." NPR (24 NOV), citing a US official, a former military cyber operator and an industry executive, reports intelligence that up to 50 commercial vessels were compromised. A US official said attackers used known vulnerabilities in Fortinet FortiGate devices and then pivoted from IT into operational technology, and "could have impacted the systems on board that control propulsion, steering, navigation, and cargo management," though no example of that was given. At least one affected ship was an LNG tanker. The Coast Guard and FBI had warned the industry about FortiGate exploitation in a September bulletin. "These are not sophisticated attacks," the official said. Fox, citing the WSJ, adds that crews received bomb threats ("traitor ships will burn in hell"), that a ransom letter to the captain of the Liberian-flagged supertanker Prosperity (about 2.3M barrels) called the operation a "planned execution," and that one ship lost communications for about 30 hours. NPR ties the campaign to suspected Iranian attacks on US water systems in August and on refrigeration systems (including on US military bases) as recently as early November.

Ahead of the holiday shipping peak, expect more Coast Guard cyber boardings and possible delays for inbound tankers and LNG carriers. Gulf Coast energy terminals are the most exposed.
