## ITEM 1: Azure API Management Classic Flaw Exploited to Steal Secrets — Microsoft Found It 18 NOV and Mitigated 19 NOV; Norway's NSM Names Equinor and Skagerak Energi; Customers Must Rotate APIM Secrets and Hunt; No CVE or Public Advisory Yet

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: The Register (0629 CST 23 NOV) says it has seen a **nonpublic Microsoft critical advisory**. The advisory says attackers raided Azure API Management instances for secrets and performed admin operations, affecting **Classic tiers only, not APIM v2**. Microsoft: "an unauthorized party could have used the issue to access secrets stored in Azure API Management Classic instances before Microsoft identified the security issue." Microsoft found the issue **18 NOV** and automatically applied a mitigation **19 NOV**. Customers must still investigate whether their instances were compromised before then. Microsoft links exposure to Classic configurations that didn't follow its network-security guidance or that stored secrets directly in APIM instead of referencing Azure Key Vault. Secrets listed as potentially exposed: named values, identity-provider and OpenID Connect client secrets, subscription keys, and tokens for the direct management API. Norway's **NSM** advisory (published 20 NOV, updated 23 NOV) says an unknown actor "actively went after access tokens, secrets and named values." NSM tells every APIM customer to assume its secrets and named values are exposed and to rotate all tokens and secrets. To verify the patch, open `https://<instance>/internal-status-0123456789abcdef` and read RuntimeVersion: **0.52.x is patched at 31936 or higher, 0.53.x at 31938 or higher**. Other version patterns are v2 or Consumption tier and are not affected. NSM names **Equinor and Skagerak Energi** among the victims and says the flaw "affects several sectors in Norway and also beyond Norway's borders." Exploitation scope is unknown. Equinor's own timeline (via SECNEWS) reported an attack 17–18 NOV that was stopped by 20 NOV, with no sign of data theft and production normal.
- IMPACT ASSESSMENT: High for any tenant still running APIM Developer, Basic, Standard or Premium (Classic). APIM sits in front of back-end APIs, so a stolen back-end credential or app-registration secret works **outside APIM** against the protected service. Forgotten dev and test instances are the likely blind spot (The Register's customer sources). It's Microsoft's second cloud-side security incident disclosed in a week, after the Entra Connect Health breach. US exposure is likely but unquantified.
- ACTION FOR ADMINS: Inventory every APIM instance across all subscriptions, dev/test included (Azure Resource Graph: `resources | where type =~ 'microsoft.apimanagement/service'`). Check RuntimeVersion against NSM's thresholds. Rotate named values, back-end credentials, IdP/OIDC client secrets, subscription keys, tenant access keys **and the Key Vault secrets APIM references**, and rotate at the source, not just in APIM. Review APIM activity and gateway logs, Entra sign-ins for the related app registrations, and back-end service logs from early November onward. Move remaining inline secrets to Key Vault references and plan migration to v2 tiers.
- SOURCE SYNOPSIS: The Register (read directly). NSM advisory (search excerpt in Norwegian; translated here). Anders Kristiansen's blog and SECNEWS (search excerpts). Microsoft's advisory is not public and was not read. No CVE identifier was found.
- FRAMING (TRADE/MAINSTREAM): The Register: "Norway links energy firm cyberattacks to critical Azure API Management flaw … admins must investigate possible compromise and rotate secrets." SECNEWS: "We could not find a CVE identifier or a Microsoft security advisory describing the flaw."
- FRAMING (COMMUNITY/ALT: HN, Reddit, X, forums): No HN thread above 60 points yet. Reddit was not reachable (HTTP 403). Kristiansen's practitioner post: "patching isn't the end of it. Assume what was stored in APIM is out."
- CONFIDENCE: HIGH that the flaw was exploited and the patch thresholds are correct (NSM, plus Microsoft as quoted by The Register). LOW on scale, actor and US victims.

## ITEM 4: vCenter CVE-2026-123890 — KEV Deadline TODAY; GTIG: ARMORY CASTLE Exploited It in Early September With "BONESAW" (Also Seen in July on a Host Patched for CVE-2026-59309); Broadcom: vSphere 8 Perpetual Customers Can Still Get Zero-Day Patches by Contacting Broadcom; 7.0 Still No Public Fix

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: The Register (2239 CST 22 NOV) covered Google Threat Intelligence Group's 19 NOV report. GTIG says ARMORY CASTLE (formerly UNC5221, suspected PRC-nexus) exploited CVE-2026-123890 in **early September 2026**. The vulnerability is an unauthenticated SRP-6a session-key disclosure over vmdird DCE/RPC (**TCP 2012**) that yields an `administrator@vsphere.local` session. The attackers got in through BeyondTrust Remote Support (CVE-2026-102764), moved to Linux appliances without EDR, scanned for ports 9080, 5480 and 2012, and then used **BONESAW** to dump the vmdir LMDB (data.mdb), hijack the SAML STS and create an SSO admin. Other tools named: SLIMSAVAGE and CAGEMATCH webshells, the BEARBONES backdoor and the SAWSNAKE SAML utility. The attackers then added an SSH key to ESXi root and stole data from VMFS. The Register says Google also found BONESAW in **July** on a server that had been patched for an earlier vCenter auth bypass, CVE-2026-59309. Broadcom's spokesperson answered the timeline questions by pointing to the VMSA Q&A and saying customers "should assume any publicly disclosed critical vulnerability can and will be exploited." The Register noticed Hock Tan's blog post no longer mentions free zero-day patches. Broadcom says the policy "remains in place" for **vSphere 8 perpetual licenses without support, by contacting Broadcom, through end of general support 11 OCT 2027**. Fixes: 8.0 U3m, 9.0.2.0200, 9.1.1.0200, VCF 5.2.5. vCenter **7.0: "Contact Broadcom Support"** for extended-support customers only (NY ITS summary of VMSA-2026-0009.1).
- IMPACT ASSESSMENT: Critical. The **KEV due date is today, MON 23 NOV**. Because exploitation goes back to at least September, a patch alone doesn't clear a vCenter that was exposed to an untrusted network segment. A Golden SAML-style token forgery can survive patching. 7.0 shops without extended support have no fix.
- ACTION FOR ADMINS: Patch today. Restrict TCP 2012, 5480 and 9080 to management jump hosts only. Hunt for unexpected SSO admin accounts, new ESXi root `authorized_keys` entries and data.mdb access. Check vmdir/STS logs back to **July**. If compromise is suspected, rotate the STS signing certificate and vsphere.local credentials. Check BeyondTrust RS/PRA appliances for CVE-2026-102764 exposure. If you run vSphere 8 perpetual without support, open a Broadcom case now for the zero-day patch.
- SOURCE SYNOPSIS: The Register (read directly). Google Cloud/GTIG blog (search excerpt). SecurityWeek, ZeroPath and NY ITS (search excerpts). Broadcom statements as quoted by The Register.
- FRAMING (TRADE/MAINSTREAM): The Register: "Google claims last week's critical VMware bug was under attack in September."
- FRAMING (COMMUNITY/ALT: HN, Reddit, X, forums): No HN thread above 60 points. Reddit was not reachable (HTTP 403). ctxsec published a community hunt guide for port-2012 scans and Golden SAML (search excerpt).
- CONFIDENCE: HIGH.

## ITEM 5: GL.iNet Confirms GoodCloud Cloud-Management Flaw Was Exploited to Plant Malware on Routers and KVMs — Security Firmware Released; IoC `crond -i /tmp/.log`; Researchers Report Reverse Shells and an Older Backdoor; Unknown Device Count

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: GL.iNet's notice (20 NOV) says that on 20 NOV it found a vulnerability in its cloud remote-management services that "an unauthorized party exploited … to access and install malicious software on some GL.iNet routers and KVM devices." The cloud path is closed, but "the cloud-side fix alone does not remove malware." Owners must install model-specific security firmware, which GL.iNet says removes the identified malware without a reset. Router examples: MT3000, MT6000 and MG1300 at 4.11.1+; BE3600 and BE9300 at 4.10.2+; AX1800, AXT1800 and X3000 at 4.8.4+; MT2500 at 4.7.14+. KVM examples: RM1, RM10 and RM4PE at 1.10.3+; RMQ1 (Comet Q) at 1.8.7+. The IoC is a process `crond -i /tmp/.log` (check with `ps | grep tmp` or LuCI Status → Processes). BleepingComputer (1245 CST 22 NOV) cites community evidence of custom malware and root reverse shells using `nc` to **170.39.226[.]219**, and says GL.iNet's check may miss other backdoors. GL.iNet's official support instructions link a community scanner. SecurityWeek (0726 CST 23 NOV) says RemoteToHome Consulting found that most of the roughly 200 routers it manages appeared affected, either by this attack or by an **older backdoor dating to about September 2025**. RemoteToHome attributes the issue to GoodCloud running cloud-supplied commands without validation, which GL.iNet hasn't confirmed. HN commenters say only devices with the **cloud service manually enabled** are exposed. GL.iNet hasn't stated that clearly.
- IMPACT ASSESSMENT: High for homelabs, travel-router users and small offices. **KVM compromise means potential BIOS-level control of attached servers and PCs.** GL.iNet says it is investigating whether KVM-attached computers were compromised. OpenWrt-flashed routers without GoodCloud aren't affected by this path.
- ACTION FOR ADMINS: For any GL.iNet router or Comet KVM (including Kyle's own): check for the IoC, disable GoodCloud and remote cloud access, and install the security firmware. On suspected infection, preserve evidence, then reflash **without keeping settings**. Rotate Wi-Fi, VPN (WireGuard/OpenVPN keys), admin and any credentials typed through a KVM. Treat KVM-attached hosts as suspect and check their startup items, accounts and scheduled tasks. Block 170.39.226.219 at the edge.
- SOURCE SYNOPSIS: GL.iNet notice, BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek and HN thread (all read directly). X news summary (claims of "command injection … present since December 2024" and a country breakdown of US, Japan, Italy and Germany; both unverified, see Quarantined).
- FRAMING (TRADE/MAINSTREAM): BleepingComputer: "Hackers exploit GL.iNet cloud flaw to infect routers, KVM devices." SecurityWeek: "GL.iNet Routers, KVMs Compromised via Remote Management Service Flaw."
- FRAMING (COMMUNITY/ALT: HN, Reddit, X, forums): HN (229 points, 106 comments): "Always disable cloud/remote access on devices like this," and a reminder that OpenWrt covers the routers "but that's not the case for the KVMs, which is quite concerning." Users on the GL.iNet forum say the official check is incomplete (via BleepingComputer).
- CONFIDENCE: HIGH that exploitation happened and on the remediation steps (vendor). MEDIUM on scope and root cause.
