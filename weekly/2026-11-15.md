## ITEM 4: Aviation — FAA nationwide ground stop of more than four hours (13 NOV) after an L3Harris copper-to-fiber router "flooded" the ATC network; ~10,000 cancellations; routers in El Dorado, Ark., and Amarillo, Texas, under test; root cause not established (Significance: HIGH)

- WHAT / WHERE / WHEN: 13–15 NOV nationwide. On Fri 13 NOV the FAA "issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following communication issues impacting multiple Air Route Traffic Control Centers." Duffy said trouble began at 3:36 p.m. (ET assumed; about 1436 CST) when "Our switches, our routers actually got overloaded with data"; airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. ET (1915 CST). Reuters called it the longest shutdown since 11 SEP 2001; by late Friday nearly 10,000 flights were canceled (Flightradar24), more than 3,500 airborne aircraft were affected across all 22 centers, and at MSP 126 of 428 departures were canceled (Cirium via Star Tribune). At a 14 NOV press conference Duffy said a contractor, L3Harris, which "actually owns that network," was switching lines from copper to fiber at "at least one" remote FAA site when the router being installed, "either through contractor error or a faulty piece of equipment," began "flooding other routers" with "over 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds" (DOT transcript). FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said there was "zero evidence" of anything "nefarious," but "Because of our lack of identifying the root cause, we were unwilling to take any risk" (CNBC). DHS Secretary Mullin said there was "zero evidence" of a cyberattack and "I don't see any reason to connect" it with the Microsoft outage. L3Harris said it is "supporting the FAA in its investigation" and that more than 70% of the conversion is complete. More than 2,500 US flights were canceled Saturday and MSP logged 224 cancellations by Saturday evening. Fresh 15 NOV: on five Sunday shows Duffy said two routers installed in recent weeks, at El Dorado, Ark., and Amarillo, Texas, are under "deep diagnostics" at L3Harris's Florida headquarters with FAA, Juniper and Cisco engineers; after the El Dorado wire was replaced and the router turned on, a problem cropped up at Amarillo, cascading into the stop: "We didn't think that should or would happen" (AP via Las Vegas Sun). "We think it was the installation or the router that was the bad actor," Duffy told Fox, adding that "President Trump actually had the whole of government on the phone on Friday" to check for foreign "footprints" and "We have no indication that that was at play here." FlightAware showed 3,798 delays and 1,638 cancellations as of noon ET Sunday (Fox News). Trump, silent publicly until Sunday, posted that "If Congress approves the desperately needed money for Air Traffic Control, we can get it built quickly," that "Much of our current air control system is 50 years old - RIDICULOUS!," and blamed the Biden administration (AP). Duffy wants billions for a "brand new architecture" telecom network (AT&T, Verizon and Lumen ready "tomorrow"); he had called for $50 billion, and the White House and DOT requested $30.1 billion for airports and ATC on 13 NOV. Democrats say billions already allocated are unspent; Duffy says those funds were designated for radar, radios and flight strips. Separately, a Copa 737 and a UPS 767 clipped wings on a Miami taxiway on 14 NOV (two minor injuries; no link to the ATC failure).
- US IMPACT ASSESSMENT: One router at a remote site halted the national airspace for more than four hours, with recovery stretching to Monday and Thanksgiving travel starting in about 10 days. The working explanation points to contractor change management on a network the FAA does not own, which will drive oversight of the L3Harris contract and a funding fight in the lame duck (11 DEC deadline) and the new Congress.

- MEDIA FRAMING (LEFT/MAINSTREAM): Reuters called it "the latest and most dramatic sign of strain" on an aging system; NPR said officials "put the blame squarely" on L3Harris; CNBC headlined that the root cause is unknown; AP stressed Trump's two-day silence and Democrats' point about unspent funds.
